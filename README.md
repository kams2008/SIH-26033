## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Agricultural products in India pass through several stages before reaching consumers. Farmers often depend on commission agents, wholesalers, distributors and retailers to sell their produce. Each intermediary may add handling, transportation, storage and commission costs, increasing the final price paid by consumers.

At the same time, farmers frequently receive a comparatively low selling price due to limited market access, lack of price information, poor bargaining power and dependence on intermediaries.

Small and marginal farmers face additional difficulties because their individual produce quantities may not be sufficient to attract large buyers. Inadequate transportation facilities, inefficient distribution and post-harvest losses further reduce their earnings.

The major challenges identified are:
- Multiple intermediaries increase the overall cost of agricultural distribution.
- Farmers lack access to transparent and updated market-price information.
- Small farmers struggle to connect directly with bulk buyers and retailers.
- Individual transportation arrangements increase logistics expenses.
- Delays in distribution contribute to food wastage and reduced product quality.
- Consumers have limited visibility into the journey and pricing of agricultural products.
The core problem is the gap between the price received by farmers and the price paid by consumers. An efficient, transparent and technology-driven agricultural supply chain is required to reduce unnecessary costs while ensuring fair opportunities for farmers and affordable prices for consumers.

2. Proposed Solution
The proposed solution, FarmLink – A Smart Collective Agricultural Marketplace, is a digital platform designed to connect farmers, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), retailers, bulk buyers and consumers through a transparent agricultural supply chain. Unlike conventional agricultural marketplaces that primarily focus on connecting individual sellers with buyers, FarmLink introduces a collective selling and intelligent distribution model to improve the efficiency of agricultural trade.

The proposed platform consists of the following key features:

1. Smart Collective Basket

FarmLink allows small farmers cultivating similar crops in nearby locations to combine their available produce into a single bulk listing. This increases their collective selling capacity, strengthens their bargaining power and makes it easier to attract supermarkets, retailers and institutional buyers.

2. PricePulse – Intelligent Price Comparison

The platform provides farmers with available market prices, nearby mandi rates, historical price trends and buyer offers. This enables farmers to compare selling opportunities and make informed pricing decisions instead of depending entirely on intermediaries.

3. Direct Deal Connect

Farmers and FPOs can connect directly with consumers, retailers, restaurants and bulk buyers. Buyers can view crop availability, quantity, quality and prices before placing orders. Farmers can accept or negotiate offers through the platform.

4. Smart Shared Logistics

The platform identifies nearby farmers and buyers with compatible delivery requirements and suggests opportunities for shared transportation. This reduces unnecessary individual transportation arrangements and improves distribution efficiency.

5. FairShare Meter – Transparent Price Breakdown

A unique feature that displays how the final consumer price is distributed across different stages, including the farmer's selling price, aggregation, handling, transportation and retail costs. This provides better visibility into the agricultural supply chain and builds trust between farmers and consumers.

6. Farm-to-Table QR Tracking

Every participating produce listing can have a unique QR code that provides information about its origin, farmer group, harvest details and available transaction history. Consumers can scan the code to understand the journey of their agricultural products.

7. AI-Based Demand Forecasting

The platform can analyze historical sales, seasonal trends and buyer demand to estimate future crop requirements. This can help farmers make more informed decisions about crop planning and reduce the possibility of unsold produce.

Innovation and Uniqueness:

FarmLink combines collective farmer aggregation, transparent price distribution and shared logistics into a single platform. Its main innovation is transforming individual small-scale agricultural sellers into a coordinated supply network without unnecessarily removing useful services such as transportation and storage.

The platform aims to reduce avoidable intermediaries while retaining essential supply-chain functions.
## Technical Approach
FarmLink will be developed as a responsive web application with separate dashboards for farmers, buyers and administrators.

A. Technologies Used
Technology	              Purpose
HTML, CSS, JavaScript	 Frontend development
Python, Django	       Backend development
SQLite / MySQL	       Database management
Python libraries	     Price and demand analysis
Maps API / OpenStreetMap	Location and route suggestions
QR Code Library	        Product traceability

B. Implementation Methodology
1. Registration: Farmers and buyers create accounts and enter their details.
2. Crop Listing: Farmers upload crop names, quantities, quality and expected prices.
3. Produce Aggregation: Nearby farmers' listings are combined into collective baskets.
4. Price Analysis: The system compares market prices and available buyer offers.
5. Buyer Matching: Buyers are matched with farmers based on crop, quantity, location and price.
6. Logistics: Compatible deliveries are grouped and shared transportation is suggested.
7. Tracking: QR codes and transaction records provide product traceability and price transparency.

C. Working Prototype

The initial prototype will include farmer registration, crop listing, collective baskets, price comparison, buyer orders, FairShare Meter and QR code generation. Advanced demand forecasting and route optimization will be introduced in later stages.

## Research and References
The proposed solution is supported by government research and existing agricultural initiatives.
1. RBI – Dynamics of Agriculture Supply Chain (2025):
A February 2025 RBI study found that farmers' share in consumer prices ranged from 40% to 67% across selected crops, highlighting the need for improved price transparency and supply-chain efficiency. 
2. NABCONS – Post-Harvest Loss Study:
A study commissioned by the Ministry of Food Processing Industries estimated vegetable post-harvest losses at 4.87%–11.61%, demonstrating the importance of efficient storage and transportation. 
3. e-NAM – National Agriculture Market:
A Government of India initiative providing an electronic trading platform connecting agricultural markets, farmers and buyers. 
4. RBI – Agriculture Supply Chain Study (2024):
Research examining agricultural supply-chain relationships and the importance of better market infrastructure and competition.
